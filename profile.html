Biography

An introduction to my academic background, interests in biology and technology, and the experiences and curiosity that shape my approach to scientific research.

Expand biography

I am an undergraduate student at the University of Alaska Fairbanks pursuing a Bachelor of Science in Biological Sciences with a concentration in Biomedical Science, while concurrently completing an Associate of Science in General Science.

My academic interests center on biology, biotechnology, and the application of technology to scientific research. I am particularly interested in biological systems, biomedical science, and how computational tools, data analysis, and engineering can contribute to a deeper understanding of living systems.

My curiosity about science extends beyond the classroom. Having spent much of my life around boats, mechanical work, and the natural environment, I have developed an appreciation for hands-on learning, practical problem-solving, and understanding how things work. I enjoy exploring the connections between scientific concepts and their real-world applications.

I hope to continue developing an interdisciplinary background through research, practical experience, and further education. The Natural Inquiry is a space to document that development, share my work, and explore the questions that continue to shape my interests in biology, technology, and the natural sciences.

Research interests

I am equally interested in how technology and scientific innovation can expand our ability to study and understand these systems.

Research experience

An overview of my undergraduate and independent research interests, including salmon health, marine biology, Pacific trade winds, and migratory bird patterns.

Expand research experience

My research experience includes undergraduate research at the University of Alaska Fairbanks and independent studies in marine and atmospheric science.

I am currently contributing to a project investigating Ichthyophonus infection in Yukon River Chinook salmon. My work involves developing skills in digital histology and image analysis using QuPath to examine parasites and changes in cardiac tissue.

My independent research has also included studying the Garibaldi (Hypsypops rubicundus), California's state marine fish, as well as the Pacific trade winds and their influence on oceanic conditions. A related study explored migratory bird patterns and their connections to broader atmospheric and environmental systems.

These projects reflect my interest in exploring questions across biology, marine science, and the natural world.
